# Hot Springs Distilling

## A place behind every bottle

Hot Springs Distilling brings together a family distillery, a tasting room, and a growing collection of spirits in Hot Springs, Arkansas. The design challenge was to give that collection a recognizable family resemblance while leaving room for each spirit’s personality—and to carry the same character into everything around it.

The direction draws on the place itself: waterfalls, bathhouses, river wildlife, rising steam, and a little local mischief. Expressive linework and sturdy lettering give the labels their voice. A consistent frame, clear hierarchy, and restrained color keep the collection connected.

## One family, three expressions

The collection is organized into House, Sourced, and Experimental. House gives Falls Creek, Spa City, River Duck, Valley of the Vapors, and Bear Clap their own illustrated identities. Sourced editions of Bear Clap and River Duck use charcoal and copper foil to establish a distinct material character. Spring Works makes room for small experiments through a slate palette and numbered release records.

That architecture has to work at more than one scale. The tall 750 mL labels give the illustrations room to tell a story; the compact 375 mL labels lead with typography. Folded seals, label frames, color, and type keep both formats part of the same family without shrinking every detail into unreadability.

## An identity people can take home

The illustrations also become shirts, sweatshirts, embroidered caps, and keepsake prints. These applications give the distillery a presence beyond the shelf and make Hot Springs itself part of what a visitor takes home.

Each format asks something different of the system. A print can carry a full scene; a cap needs a concise typographic signature. The shared palette, lettering, and illustration language hold those expressions together while allowing the composition to change.

## From the label to the browser

The website translates the packaging into an open, editorial experience. Houschka Pro carries the label’s distinctive letterforms into live text. A Polar White field, iron-red accents, and oversized native Bear Clap artwork establish the homepage, while generous space and simple rules keep the spirits and visitor information easy to read.

The SvelteKit build gives each product and edition its own page. House and Sourced remain distinct even when they share a name. Spring Works becomes an open notebook with individual experiment records. Permanent product paths provide specific destinations for bottle QR codes, connecting the physical identity to a useful digital experience.

## A system that can keep growing

The website’s colors, type, spacing, and product themes come from shared tokens. Structured content records feed reusable page layouts, so a new edition or experiment can gain a page and a collection entry without a separate design. The working brand reference brings those decisions together with component examples and responsive behavior.

The content model also distinguishes verified facts from draft material. Spring Works prototypes retain visible concept labels, while visitor information carries source records. This makes the system useful for exploration without treating a proposed release or a generated mockup as evidence of a finished product.

## What I owned, and where it stands

My work connects art direction, identity and packaging development, merchandise and print applications, digital design, content architecture, and frontend engineering. The throughline is a system that can keep its character as it moves between physical objects and working software.
