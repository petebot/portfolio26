# Synchronic Studio

## The personal problem

Synchronic began with a practical problem in my own life: work and parenting had pushed painting to the edges of my schedule. I was painting rarely, even though maintaining a creative practice remained important to me.

I began building the app in August 2025 as a way to keep that practice present every day. Daily writing surfaces ideas and intentions I do not want to lose, while visible records of actual studio work make continuity easier to see. I now use Synchronic every day and paint weekly.

## Five parts of one practice

The product is organized around five pillars, loosely influenced by the methods in _The Artist's Way_ and shaped around what I need to remain consistent:

- Observe shows how consistently I have engaged with each part of the practice.
- Practice records working sessions and photographs of the work produced.
- Roam captures time spent out in the world looking for inspiration.
- Tend manages tasks and offers AI-generated guidance and nudges.
- Reflect supports daily journaling.

Together they treat inspiration, making, maintenance, and reflection as connected parts of an artist's life rather than separate productivity categories.

## Guidance that leaves the artist in charge

Tend looks for recurring signals across reflections, practice sessions, and roaming entries, then proposes grounded, specific next steps. Suggestions are intentionally gentle and practical: no productivity-hustle voice, motivational filler, or automatic changes to the artist's plans.

Every generated card remains a proposal. The artist can edit, accept, dismiss, release, or complete it. AI helps surface a thread; it does not decide what the work should become.

## The working product

The current SvelteKit application uses Supabase for accounts, structured practice data, images, reminders, and synchronization. Tauri packages the same product as a focused desktop application. Shared logic keeps daily records and cross-pillar summaries consistent between the web and desktop experiences.

The visual language reflects a long-standing personal interest in Platonic solids and sacred geometry. Warm editorial surfaces and precise controls keep the product reflective without making everyday use feel precious or slow.

## What I owned

I created the product model, five-pillar workflow, visual and interaction system, frontend architecture, Supabase data layer, desktop packaging, AI-guidance flow, testing approach, and release process. I continue to design, build, and use the product as part of the same practice it supports.

Synchronic is designed for other artists, but I am currently its only user. Opening it more broadly depends on further testing and refinement. Its most meaningful result so far is personal and concrete: it has become part of my daily life and helped turn painting from a rare event into a weekly practice.
